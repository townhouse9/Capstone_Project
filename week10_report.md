# Week 10 Critical Reflection Report: Methodology, Transparency, and Limitations

### 1. Guided Reasoning & Historical Patterns
Round 10 strategy was guided by aggressive boundary exploitation and active subspace tracking. In Function 5, successive rounds revealed an exponential ascending ridge towards the upper hypercube limits; querying the vertex (1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0) yielded an unprecedented output of 8662.48. In Function 8, Automatic Relevance Determination (ARD) identified coordinates x1 and x2 as near-dormant, whereas x5 exhibited acute sensitivity, allowing targeted candidate sampling that unlocked an all-time record of 9.956667. For Function 4, following a catastrophic valley drop in Round 8 (-36.65), we restricted candidates to the narrow positive peak island around (0.40, 0.38, 0.39, 0.39), successfully restoring positive yield (0.013954). Functions 2 and 6 also achieved new global maxima (0.676790 and -0.208432) through refined local exploitation.

### 2. Transparency and Reproducibility
Our decision-making process is fully deterministic, transparent, and reproducible. Every query proposal is generated through automated 5-fold cross-validation benchmarking eight regression families (three ARD Gaussian Process kernels, Random Forests, Extra Trees, Gradient Boosting, Polynomial Ridge, and Multi-Layer Perceptrons). All historical arrays, random seeds (fixed at 42), candidate generation distributions (70% Latin Hypercube, 30% local Gaussian perturbation), and Expected Improvement acquisition calculations are version-controlled in our GitHub repository. An external researcher would only require our initial NumPy arrays, our bbo.py engine, and the logged scikit-learn hyperparameter bounds to replicate every proposed query identically.

### 3. Core Assumptions and Result Shaping
Our primary technical assumption is local stationarity and spatial continuity within coordinate sub-regions, modelled through Matérn covariance kernels. We assume that high-yield outputs are clustered in continuous topological basins or monotonic ridges rather than isolated Dirac-like spikes. This assumption enabled rapid ascent along confirmed gradients in Functions 5 and 8. However, this assumption inherently shapes and limits our results: if any objective contains deceptive, disjointed multi-modal spikes isolated in low-performing regions, our surrogate will dismiss those regions as unpromising and prematurely over-exploit an inferior local mode.

### 4. Dataset Gaps and Sampling Biases
Given our strict budget of one physical evaluation per function per week, our dataset exhibits severe exploitation bias. In high-dimensional spaces (Function 7 in 6D and Function 8 in 8D), over 75% of the hypercube volume remains completely unexplored. Our queries have clustered tightly around historical high points and domain boundaries. While this concentrated sampling successfully maximized our observed maximums, it created substantial epistemic blind spots across interior regions where alternative global optima may lie hidden.

### 5. Significant Limitations
The most significant limitation of our approach is surrogate model over-reliance under extreme sample sparsity. In 8 dimensions with only 49 evaluated points, fitting anisotropic ARD lengthscales risks over-fitting to early points. If the true black-box function contains abrupt non-stationary noise or sharp cliffs, Gaussian Processes can underestimate posterior variance, leading to overconfident predictions that either overlook promising voids or become trapped in local boundaries.
