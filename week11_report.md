### 3. Ineffective Parameter Choices and Resulting Strategic Adjustments
Early rounds demonstrated that uniform random sampling and isotropic Gaussian Process kernels with uniform scalar lengthscales fail completely in high dimensions because the curse of dimensionality leaves candidate pools excessively sparse and unable to detect anisotropic ridges. Furthermore, aggressive unconstrained global exploration in volatile landscapes—such as the exploratory step in Function 4 that previously plummeted to -36.65—wasted valuable evaluation budget without yielding informative gradient signals. To counter these vulnerabilities, we phased out isotropic kernels in favour of anisotropic ARD covariance structures, implemented a hybrid candidate generator allocating seventy per cent to space-filling Latin Hypercubes and thirty per cent to local Gaussian perturbations, and enforced a minimum Euclidean distance threshold of 0.005 to prevent wasteful point duplication.

The acquisition engine acts analogously to a centroid-updating mechanism, calculating the mathematical expectation of improvement to shift the query distribution towards the densest regions of high-yield probability while tightening trust-region boundaries around confirmed modes.
